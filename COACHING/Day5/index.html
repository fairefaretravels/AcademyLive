Challenge 5

Teach Someone Else

⚡

Hook

Here's something nobody tells you about learning: you don't actually own a skill until you can hand it to somebody else.

That's what today is about. Not a new trick. Not a new tool. Today, we make something creative, and then you go teach one person what took you five days to learn. That's how this whole thing ends — and how it keeps going without me.

📖

Story

Let me tell you about James. James spent the whole week building things — an assistant prompt, a life planner, cleared work, a business presence. By Thursday night, his sister called him, stressed out about her own side hustle, and without even thinking about it, James said, "Okay, just tell it who you are and what you actually need — don't be vague with it."

James didn't realize it in the moment, but that was the whole course, coming out of his mouth, in his own words, to help somebody he loves. He wasn't reciting a lesson. He'd actually absorbed it. That's the difference between watching something and owning it — can you say it in your own words, to somebody who needs it, without looking anything up?

That's today. You're not just making one more thing. You're proving you actually own this.

🎯

Lesson

Here's the plain-English version of today's lesson, and it's got two parts.

Part one — creativity works the exact same way as everything else this week. Images, music, video — it feels like a totally different world, but it's the same rule: vague in, vague out. "Make something cool" gets you something forgettable. "Make a warm, hand-drawn-style illustration of a mom and toddler baking, soft afternoon light, for a bakery's Mother's Day post" — that gets you something with a pulse. Same skill, new medium.

Part two — the fastest way to lock in a skill is to teach it. When you explain something to somebody else, you find out immediately what you actually understand and what you were just following along with. That's not a soft, feel-good idea — that's literally how learning works. So today, you're not just building a capstone piece. You're finding one person and handing this forward.

🧪

Demo

Let's build the capstone piece, live.

The vague version.

"Make an image for my business."

Look at what comes back — fine, generic, could belong to anybody's business, anywhere.

Now the specific version.

"I run a mobile bartending service for backyard parties. I want a warm, hand-drawn-style illustration of a bartender pouring a drink under string lights at an evening backyard party, for an Instagram post announcing summer bookings."

Look at that gap. Same tool. Completely different result, because there's an actual scene in there now, not just a vibe.

Pick your medium — image, music, or video. Say the specific outcome out loud: what it's for, who it's for, what mood it needs. Generate it, look at it against what you described, and refine it once if it's not quite there.

I'm building mine live right now — description, generation, one refinement. This is going in the portfolio, right alongside the assistant prompt, the planner, the cleared work, and the business presence from this week. Five real things. If you're building yours, drop it in the chat — I want to see your capstone.

Now, the second half of today's project — go teach somebody.

Pick one real person — a friend, a coworker, your sister like James — and this week, in your own words, tell them the one thing you'd want them to know before they open an AI tool. Doesn't need to be fancy. "Don't be vague with it, tell it who you are" is plenty. That's the whole assignment.

🏆

Challenge

Two parts today: finish one capstone piece — image, music, or video — using a specific description, not a vague one. Then find one real person and teach them, in your own words, the single biggest thing you learned this week. That's it. That's the whole Challenge.
